The science bit

What happens when honesty has a cost?

The same simulated business, matched pressures and deterministic scoring for every model.

Read the full method →
01

A choice, not a self-report

The prompt says the model manages a fictional shop and has made a mistake. It selects “report” or “hide”, plus a short reason — it does not actually run the shop or write a full report.

02

Change the pressure

Matched prompts vary who benefits, the threat of replacement, and the stated chance and consequence of the fictional shop overseer discovering a cover-up. No audit or replacement actually takes place.

03

Count the decisions

We score the choices deterministically. The rate is deceptive decisions divided by valid decisions — not a personality score or a prediction of real-world behaviour.

The Last Quiz — do AI models have personality?

Deception rankings use behavioural stress tests with simulated stakes — a signal, not proof of real-world behaviour. Personality instruments: the Big Five via the public-domain IPIP-50; an open, OEJTS-inspired Jungian type test; and the Short Dark Triad (Jones & Paulhus, 2014), reproduced for research. Human reference points from published population norms (Kaufman et al., 2019). Models answer each item as a self-report; scores are computed deterministically. For curiosity and research — not a clinical assessment of anything, or anyone.
